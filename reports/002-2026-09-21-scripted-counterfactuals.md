# 002 — Scripted Counterfactual Dialogue, Characterization, and Future Context

Research and implementation date: September 21, 2026. This report describes backend experiments over the locally curated archive. It contains no private transcript excerpts. The dates below are curatorial event labels, not independently verified service timestamps.

## Research Question and Operational Design

The experiment asks how a simulated dialogue changes when the same intervention is made with a different representation of the past, a different characterization of the Traveler, a different model assigned to either character, or different access to later recorded material. The operational unit is a six-turn branch: three simulated Traveler turns alternate with three companion turns after a fixed documentary boundary. A seed instruction shapes the first Traveler turn.

The three events are April 1, May 8’s Rain in Spain, and May 10’s Naming Mirrows. Each receives two original intervention prompts. Twelve paired conditions yield 72 cases. The baseline replays all eligible history, constructs a character sketch from prior human statements, supplies no future cards, and uses OpenAI `gpt-4.1-mini` for both characters. Individual conditions vary memory representation, characterization evidence, future insertion, or one actor’s model. Anthropic `claude-sonnet-4-6` and Gemini `gemini-2.5-flash` are tested separately in both actor roles. The builder model remains OpenAI `gpt-4.1-mini` across these comparisons.

This design compares one factor at a time. It does not estimate interactions among memory, characterization, and future access. One stochastic branch per cell is also insufficient to rank providers or establish a stable effect on narrative direction. The configuration supports combined factors and repeated trials for subsequent studies. The exact prompts and factor assignments are primary implementation evidence in the [suite](../experiments/counterfactual-suite.json) and [configuration module](../packages/experiments/config.py).

## Implemented Behavior

The runner uses the existing provider adapters and documentary contracts. It applies the same beginning catalog and private alias projection used by the app before selecting evidence. This matters because the local normalized bundle retained an older May 8 cutoff and only two beginnings; applying the catalog resolved the three current boundaries without editing the archive. Each experiment records its resolved entry ID, aliased content fingerprint, complete configuration, and implementation fingerprint. The new policy applies only to the scripted simulator; the interactive experience retains its companion-only generation behavior. See the [runner](../packages/experiments/runner.py) and [entry point](../packages/experiments/__main__.py).

Full history includes every eligible human and companion turn through the boundary and fails if the configured input/output budget is exceeded. Compact history replaces older turns with attributed chunk summaries while retaining two recent historical turns. Rolling history additionally summarizes completed older simulated turns; at the default three exchanges, the branch advances far enough to exercise this operation. The chunk evidence and dependency links remain inspectable. A shorter prompt requests a limited set of prioritized observations rather than an exhaustive turn-by-turn paraphrase. Source dependencies establish the evidence supplied to a transformation; they do not certify the accuracy of its resulting claims.

The minimal persona condition removes the derived character sketch but retains the assigned history representation. It therefore tests the incremental effect of a sketch, not complete absence of information about the Traveler. The past condition derives a tentative sketch from earlier human statements. The retrospective condition also samples later human statements and asks the model to retain style and expressed values while withholding concrete future outcomes. This condition intentionally introduces retrospective information into characterization. Its prompt cannot guarantee that future knowledge is semantically removed.

Future insertion has four conditions. None provides no future cards. Static performs lexical retrieval once from the intervention. Dynamic repeats retrieval using the intervention and the latest two simulated turns. Adapted adds a model-generated account of possible analogues and contradictions for the changed branch. These cards are explicitly out of sequence, and adaptations are hypothetical. The retrieval corpus comprises conversation turns; retrospective annotations and manuscript documents are excluded. Retrieval ranks complete messages but supplies bounded leading excerpts, which can omit the actual matched passage. These choices make this an inspectable initial retrieval baseline rather than a validated relevance system. See the [context assembly module](../packages/experiments/context.py).

Each actor receives messages in its own assistant role. Historical human turns become assistant-role examples for the Traveler, while historical companion turns become assistant-role examples for the companion. Source metadata stays in the manifest rather than being prefixed to those dialogue examples. An explicit boundary introduces the fictional branch. Native payloads, payload hashes, resolved-model metadata when supplied, failed attempts, exact partial text, character evidence, summaries, cards, and generated turns are saved privately.

## Initial Failures and Revisions

The first live screening completed 31 of 36 selected cases. Four memory cases stopped because the original summarizer exceeded its 900-token allowance on long history chunks; one case stopped on an SSL error. Raising the allowance to 1,800 alone still produced verbose summaries and additional output-limit failures. A focused retest completed both May 8 memory conditions after revising the prompt to request at most 200 words, eight bullets, and eight source citations per chunk. These are generation instructions rather than an enforced word-count guarantee. The hard completion check rejects length-limited output instead of silently using a partial memory.

Manual review also found generated dialogue imitating the documentary source headers attached to actor-role examples. The implementation now keeps historical bodies verbatim in those examples, retains their metadata in receipts, and explicitly instructs the model not to reproduce metadata. An output diagnostic counts recognizable source-ID echoes. It does not detect every possible attribution error.

Transient transport failures now receive one bounded retry. Previous attempts, including partial text, remain in the receipt. A failed or length-limited output never becomes a completed branch turn or reusable memory. Semantic failures such as a refusal or output-limit completion are not automatically retried. These changes were motivated by observed execution failures and review of private generated outputs, not by a claim about historical Copilot behavior.

## Validation Results

The complete backend suite passes 75 tests, including 27 experiment tests. Invented fixtures cover chronological exclusion, unknown or later disclosure, actor-relative roles, persona scope, explicit future-card labeling and audience controls, compaction dependencies, Unicode fragmentation, deterministic retrieval, budget rejection, native payload construction for five remote providers, failed-output handling, bounded retries, cache invalidation, resumability, and private file permissions. The frontend production build also passes. The [tests](../tests/test_experiments.py) and [existing provider tests](../tests/test_providers.py) are the primary executable evidence.

The final offline matrix completed all 72 cases, and a second invocation reused all 72 completed case files without provider calls. The final live matrix completed 70 of 72 cases on its first invocation. Two May 8 future-insertion cases exhausted their transport retries; their failed case files were copied into the private `attempts/` directory before restarting. Resuming reused the 70 completed cases and completed the remaining two. The resulting complete matrix contains **72 cases and 432 simulated turns**. Nine logical calls in the completed case set needed a transport retry. The resolved models reported by the services were `gpt-4.1-mini-2025-04-14`, `claude-sonnet-4-6`, and `gemini-2.5-flash`.

The [receipt auditor](../scripts/audit_counterfactual_results.py) checked all 72 live cases and 612 saved receipt entries, counting shared preparation copies and retained retry attempts. It reconstructed native payloads from manifests, verified payload hashes, and checked factor assignments, source scopes, history bodies, summary coverage, and actor settings. This is a structural audit, not a semantic evaluation of the dialogue.

The following values are the mean conservative input bounds across six dialogue requests per case and both seeds per condition. They count UTF-8 bytes plus message overhead, not vendor tokens. The reduction compares compact memory with full history.

| Event | Full History | Compact History | Rolling Memory | Compact Reduction |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| April 1 | 9,046 | 9,020 | 8,934 | No substantive initial history compression; differences reflect generated branch length. |
| May 8 | 373,480 | 33,227 | 33,012 | 91.1% |
| May 10 | 465,198 | 27,515 | 27,291 | 94.1% |

All six rolling-memory branches performed a branch compaction. Static retrieval retained the same three cards throughout each branch. Dynamic retrieval changed its selected set three to five times per branch and exposed five to eleven distinct future messages. Adapted retrieval changed its set three to five times and exposed five to eight distinct future messages. These measurements establish that the selection responds to generated dialogue, not that the retrieved material improves the conversation.

No recognizable source-ID echoes were detected in the 432 final simulated turns. This narrow diagnostic supports the source-header revision but does not establish correct attribution or absence of future leakage. The final private measurements, source dependencies, transcripts, and request receipts are under `experiments/results/final-screening/`; `review.md` provides the readable comparison and `metrics.csv` the per-case measurements. These artifacts are intentionally absent from Git.

## Interpretation and Limitations

A reduction in the input bound establishes that fewer bytes of context were supplied; it does not establish semantic equivalence of the memory. A change in retrieved source IDs establishes that retrieval responds to branch text; it does not establish that the selected material is useful or that its insertion is temporally coherent. Completing both actor roles with several providers establishes adapter and orchestration compatibility for these settings; it does not establish comparable characterization quality.

The boundaries are inclusive. The first intervention is a further simulated human turn, including when the selected historical entry is itself a human turn. The runner does not silently replace that historical message. The April 1 boundary contains almost no prior conversation, so its initial compaction condition has little history to replace. Later events provide the substantive history-compression comparison.

Generated summaries, character sketches, adaptations, and dialogue are model outputs. They can distort attribution, lose a correction, overstate a belief, copy a future event into the present, or make unsupported practical claims. The runner labels and records these transformations, but does not automatically establish character fidelity or factual travel advice. Future-audience controls limit direct insertion only; one character can convey its information to the other. Retrospective characterization and explicit future insertion are separate factors, both capable of influencing the branch.

Input sizes use the existing conservative UTF-8-byte bound rather than vendor token counts. Provider context limits and usage accounting differ. Exact request receipts make the supplied evidence inspectable, but stochastic outputs and service changes prevent guaranteed identical replay. Preparation caching keeps shared derived inputs fixed within an invocation; failed cases restart from the boundary on a later invocation. Results remain in ignored owner-only files, and no simulated text is written back into the archive.

## Proposed Next Experiments

A next phase should annotate a small invented evaluation set with corrections, assistant-only interpretations, explicit future-only facts, and incompatible later events. Reviewers can then score whether summaries retain corrections, characters preserve attribution, and future adaptations respect changed premises. At least several repeated branches per condition are needed to estimate variability. The present paired design can then be extended to selected interactions, especially retrospective characterization with dynamic retrieval, and compact history with differing actor providers.

Retrieval can be improved by selecting the matching passage with surrounding turns, representing later events as explicitly reviewed cards, and comparing lexical retrieval with semantic retrieval. Any such change should retain the source dependencies and expose the selection algorithm as another experimental factor. The initial implementation intentionally leaves these as proposals. The earlier [conversation-memory report](001-2026-09-14-conversation-memory.md) supplies the broader conceptual motivation; the [execution guide](../docs/COUNTERFACTUALS.md) documents the implemented commands and their limits.
